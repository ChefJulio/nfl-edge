# How the model works

## The short version

NFL Edge makes three kinds of estimates each week: how many fantasy points every quarterback, running back, receiver and tight end will score; how strong every team is; and each team's chance to win its game. Everything here is an estimate with honest error bars, not a pick or a betting tip.

**Grading every play.** Football is made of plays, so we start there. Each play is graded against what normally happens in that exact spot: a 6-yard gain on 3rd-and-5 is great, the same 6 yards on 3rd-and-10 is a failure. Credit and blame are split between the offense and the defense on the field, and adjusted for who they faced, so shredding a great defense counts for more than shredding a bad one. Recent games count most, and last season's play fades out as this one goes on. The result is a rating for every team's passing offense, running offense, pass defense, run defense and special teams. Those ratings are the **Power** rankings, shown as points per game better or worse than an average team.

**Predicting a game.** From those ratings, plus each team's usual pace, we estimate how many plays each team will run, how often it will throw, and how well it should move the ball against this particular opponent.

**Predicting a player.** A player's projection is three things multiplied together:

- *How much of the work he gets:* his share of his team's passes, carries or targets, weighted toward recent games.
- *How much the team will have:* the plays from the game prediction above.
- *How productive he is per chance:* yards and touchdowns per target or carry, adjusted for how many points Vegas expects his team to score against this opponent, the teammates around him, and strong wind. A back who gets the goal-line carries gets a touchdown boost, but only as big as his team's chances to get near the goal line.

A player with only a few games is pulled toward what is typical for players like him (same position, similar draft slot), so two hot weeks aren't treated as the new normal. As the season goes on, his own numbers take over.

**Injuries.** Practice reports (did not practice / limited / full) and game status turn into a chance he plays. If he is likely out, his share goes to teammates the way it has gone in past absences. Projections are always "if he plays", with the chance he sits shown next to it.

**Experts.** Once the weekly expert rankings come out, they are mixed in for running backs, receivers and tight ends, because testing showed the mix beats either alone. Quarterbacks stay ours alone, because the mix made them worse.

**Ranges.** Instead of one number you also get a floor and a ceiling, based on how real scores have spread around past projections like this one. About 8 times in 10 the actual score lands between them.

**Win chances.** The headline chance is the betting market's, turned into a percentage, because over years of games it has been the most accurate forecast. Next to it is our own independent opinion (team ratings, a score-based team rating, and home field) as a second look. Under {{ L.tossup }}% we call it a tossup. A 70% favorite should still lose about 3 times in 10, so an upset isn't automatically a miss.

**Why trust it.** Every part was tested on past seasons the way it would have worked then, using only information available before each game. A change only stays if that testing shows a clear improvement. The detailed sections below describe every piece, with the live formulas at the end.

## The full pipeline

NFL Edge projects weekly fantasy points for QBs, RBs, WRs and TEs and rates every team, using only free data. Every number on this site comes from the pipeline below. Values shown in **bold blue** are filled in live from the current build, so they always match what produced this week's report.

**Pipeline, start to finish:** load and cache data, then fit opponent-adjusted team ratings. From those, predict each game's volume and score, project each player's share of that volume and his efficiency, and adjust for availability and injuries. Finally, blend with the expert consensus where that has been shown to help, and attach calibrated ranges. Learned components train only on seasons *before* the one being projected or tested, mostly the **{{ L.train_seasons }}** seasons before it. The exceptions use longer history:

- the points formula: up to 6 earlier seasons;
- the practice-availability model: every season since 2018;
- the injury-return model: every loaded season, including this season's finished absences;
- the Vegas win-chance curve and Elo: every earlier game.

Team ratings use this season's completed games plus a fading share of last season's. A few settings are fixed values chosen by testing on 2021-2026 history: the win-chance blend's weights, the tossup line, and tuned knobs such as half-lives and the wind penalty.

## 2. Team ratings

Each play's EPA (how much the play changed the expected score) is modeled as

> EPA = league average + offense strength + defense strength + home edge (+ the quarterback's own effect, for passing)

fit separately for **passing** (dropbacks, including sacks and scrambles), **rushing** (designed runs) and **special teams** (punts, kickoffs, field goals, extra points). Only regular-season plays are used. Kneels, spikes and two-point tries are removed. Garbage time is also removed: passing and rushing plays count only when the offense's win probability is between **{{ L.wp_min }}** and **{{ L.wp_max }}**. Special teams has no garbage-time filter.

- **Opponent adjustment.** Solving for all teams at once (ridge regression) means a defense that faced weak offenses gets less credit than its raw numbers suggest. Ridge regression shrinks each team, and the home edge, toward average by an amount (alpha) chosen by **time-split cross-validation** on the training seasons: for each week from week 4 on, fit on everything before it, predict each team's EPA per play that week, and never shuffle. Current alphas: pass **{{ L.alpha_pass }}**, rush **{{ L.alpha_rush }}**, special teams **{{ L.alpha_st }}**.
- **Recency.** Plays from this season are weighted with a half-life of **{{ L.hl_team }}** weeks. Last season's plays all carry one flat weight: full weight for week-1 projections (they are all there is), then **{{ L.prior_weight }}** x (**{{ L.prior_decay }}** - week) / ({{ L.prior_decay }} - 1), which is about 0.26 at week 2 and zero by week **{{ L.prior_decay }}**. Older seasons are not used.
- **Quarterbacks.** Passing offense = the team (line, receivers, scheme) + its quarterback + a league-wide effect for inexperienced quarterbacks (fewer than **{{ L.qb_cut }}** career dropbacks, counting pass attempts back to 2006). Its size and sign are learned, not assumed. The team and QB shrinkage amounts are tuned jointly; the QB grid includes "no QB effects at all", so cross-validation could reject them. QB shrinkage alpha **{{ L.alpha_qb }}**, inexperienced-QB effect **{{ L.qb_inexp }}** points per 100 dropbacks. Highest QB effects among quarterbacks with any loaded history (not only current starters): {{ L.top_qbs }}.
- **Home field.** One league-wide edge per unit (passing, rushing and special teams), fit inside the same weighted regression as the ratings. Currently pass **{{ L.home_pass }}**, rush **{{ L.home_rush }}** points per 100 plays. Neutral-site games get none. A division-game home term was tested and is off.
- **Certainty.** Ratings are refit on **{{ L.bootstrap }}** resamples of games (a cluster bootstrap) to measure each passing and running unit's uncertainty. The Power tab simulates each team's net rating from those spreads and shows the middle 80% of simulated ranks. Special-teams and QB uncertainty are not included.

Ratings are estimated per play. On the Power tab they are shown as **points per game versus an average team on a neutral field**: per-play rating x a league-average number of those plays x the points formula's points-per-EPA weight (section 3). That way the units add up. Values per 100 plays appear on hover. Higher is better for both offense and defense, because the defense number is flipped so "good" always reads positive.

**Field position is already accounted for.** EPA judges every play against what was expected from that exact down, distance and field position. A defense handed a short field by its offense's turnover starts the drive already "owing" the high expected points of that spot, and is charged only for what happens beyond that; the turnover itself counts against the offense that committed it. Raw points-allowed would wrongly blame the defense.

## 3. Game lines

### Volume and points

**Volume.** Each team's expected plays come from a regression on its recent plays per game and the plays its opponent allows. Pass rate comes from three things:

- the team's recent pass rate;
- its neutral-situation pass rate over expected (PROE: win probability 20-80%, quarters 1-3);
- game script: the Vegas spread when lines are posted, otherwise the model's own projected margin.

Player projections also use the Vegas total and spread when every game that week has a line. Matchups always uses the model-only version.

**Points.** Expected offensive EPA per game (dropbacks × pass EPA + rushes × rush EPA, against this opponent, including home field and the starting QB), plus the special-teams edge, converts to points through a calibration learned on the 6 seasons before: {{ L.points_model }}. It uses a longer window than other components because two seasons pin down the special-teams weight poorly.

**The model publishes win chances only.** Point spreads on the site are always the market's. The model's own margin (team ratings, projected starting QBs and pace; never Vegas) is never shown. It only sets game script for the projected pass rate, and the build checks that the battle-bar impacts add up to it.

### Battle bars (Matchups)

These break the matchup down in EPA per 100 plays:

- each offense's rating minus the opposing defense's, with the total weighting passing and running by that offense's projected dropbacks and runs;
- special teams per 100 special-teams plays;
- the home-field edge per scrimmage play.

Pace is listed as projected plays and pass rate. Bar length is the edge's impact on the game through the points formula, on one scale for every row:

- offense rows: edge x projected plays x points per EPA;
- special teams: edge x league-average special-teams plays x twice the fitted special-teams weight.

A full bar is the 95th percentile of offense-vs-defense impact across every pairing of that week's ratings. So equal numbers can draw different bars, and each special-teams play counts more than a scrimmage play because of field position.

### Win chances

- **Vegas win chances.** The spread becomes a win probability through P(win) = 1 / (1 + e^(-k x spread)), with k fit every run on every earlier finished game with a line (playoffs included, ties excluded).
- **Our model's win chance** = 1 / (1 + e^(-x)), where x = **{{ L.wm_home }}** for the home team (0 at a neutral site) + **{{ L.wm_rank_gap }}** per Power-rank place of advantage + **{{ L.wm_elo_per_100 }}** per 100 points of Elo advantage.
- **Elo** is a rating from final scores only, playoffs included. Every team starts at 1505 in the first loaded season. After each game it moves by K = **{{ L.wm_elo_k }}** x (result - expected) x a margin multiplier, ln(|margin| + 1) x 2.2 / (winner's Elo edge x 0.001 + 2.2), so bigger wins move it more but a favorite's expected wins move it less. The expected result counts **{{ L.wm_elo_hfa }}** Elo points of home field (none at neutral sites). Each offseason, ratings are pulled **{{ L.wm_elo_revert }}** of the way back to 1505.
- **How the blend was chosen.** The live Elo settings and the three weights are fit once by `tools/elo_eval.py` on every season through 2026 and stored in configuration. The backtest refits them each test year on earlier seasons only. Elo's settings were stable across those years. Walk-forward on 2022-26 games with a line (Brier, lower = better): this blend 0.2228, Power rank + home 0.2292, the model's margin alone 0.2353, Vegas 0.2111, always the home team 0.249. Elo adds what the play-by-play ratings miss: actual results and memory across seasons. On a larger sample (`tools/rank_predictor.py`), the rank also beat the rating gap it comes from (0.2291 vs 0.2316), because the size of extreme rating gaps is unreliable. Nothing tested has added anything to the market's own chance (`tools/darts.py`: 28 pre-game signals including QB tiers, rest, travel, cold weather and referees). So the Matchups headline is the market's chance whenever a line exists, and the model's only before lines post.
- **Tossups.** A game is a tossup when the favorite's chance, rounded, is under **{{ L.tossup }}%**. That is the largest cut-off below which the market's favorites won at a rate a coin flip explains (54% +/- 6%, 90% interval, on 2022-26). It falls at a spread under 3 points; 3-point favorites won about 61%. The track record at the end of this page shows, for every confidence level, how often the favorite actually won, using chances fit only on earlier seasons.

### Playoff odds (Teams > Playoffs)

- **Method.** `tools/playoff_odds.py` plays out the rest of the season 10,000 times. Finished games count as played. Each remaining game uses the Matchups chance: the market's when a line is posted, otherwise our model's (above), with ranks and Elo held where they are now. Every simulated season is seeded with the NFL tiebreakers (`nfledge/standings.py`) and the playoffs are played to the end in the real format (the 1 seed's bye, reseeding after the wild-card round, home field to the higher seed, a neutral-site Super Bowl).
- **Drift.** Team strength isn't frozen for the rest of a season: injuries, trades and form all move it. Each simulated season gives every team one strength shift, drawn from a normal distribution with standard deviation 0.9 x sqrt(share of the season left) on the log-odds scale, and applies it to all of that team's remaining games. Without drift the odds were overconfident: teams given 85-95% made the playoffs 76% of the time in 2024-25. The size was pre-registered and chosen on 2021-23 from 0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 and 1.5. On 2024-25, which the fit never saw, it lowered the make-playoffs Brier from 0.1344 to 0.1273.
- **Track record** (walk-forward, 2021-25, odds as of every week, using that week's Power ranks and lines; 1,000 simulated seasons per week): make-playoffs Brier 0.138 vs 0.246 for the base rate; win the division 0.118 vs 0.188; first-round bye 0.038 vs 0.059; win the Super Bowl 0.028 vs 0.030 (barely better than the base rate: one champion in 32 is hard to call). Calibration: each band of predicted chances from 0-5% to 95-100% made the playoffs within 2.3 points of its average prediction. The odds sharpen as the season goes: Brier 0.206 in weeks 1-4, 0.067 in weeks 15-18. The Playoffs tab shows the full table.

## 4. Player projections

**Projection = projected volume × efficiency per opportunity**, computed stat by stat (passing yards, TDs and interceptions; rushing yards and TDs; catches, receiving yards and TDs; fumbles) and then scored with the scoring file (default half-PPR).

**Volume.** Usage shares are taken over games the player actually played: dropbacks for QBs, share of designed carries, and targets per team dropback.

- **Recency.** This season's games are weighted with a half-life of **{{ L.hl_share }}** weeks. Last season's games carry the same flat, fading weight as in the team ratings.
- **Regression to the mean.** A learned calibration pulls each share toward the player's longer-run average, because recent spikes partly regress.
- **Team totals.** Each team's projected carries and targets are then pulled toward the totals a real game holds: carries 70% of the way, targets 30%.
- **Quarterbacks.** The depth-chart starter gets at least 96% of dropbacks. QB rushes = designed carries + scramble rate x dropbacks.

The team's projected dropbacks and rushes then turn shares into opportunities.

**Efficiency.** For each stat per opportunity (yards per target, catch rate, TD rate and so on):

1. *Empirical-Bayes shrinkage.* The player's rate, over this season and the two before (weighted 1 / 0.6 / 0.3), is pulled toward the average for his position and draft tier (round 1, rounds 2-3, rounds 4-7, undrafted; a tier with too little data falls back to the position average). How hard depends on how noisy that stat is, estimated from data as a "prior strength" k in pseudo-opportunities. Examples: WR catch rate k ≈ **{{ L.k_wr_catch }}**, WR yards per target k ≈ **{{ L.k_wr_ypt }}**, RB yards per carry k ≈ **{{ L.k_rb_ypc }}**. A small k means the stat is mostly real skill; a large k means it is mostly noise.
2. *Expected touchdowns.* Instead of raw TDs, each touch and target carries the historical TD rate for its field position (and end-zone targets). The player's TD rate blends toward his actual TDs as his sample grows (weight n / (n + **{{ L.td_k }}**)).
3. *Learned efficiency regressions* combine the shrunk rate; the player's opponent-adjusted EPA; his own unit's offensive rating, i.e. the supporting cast (pass offense with the projected starting QB for passing and receiving stats, run offense for rushing); home field; and the Vegas implied team total (the model's own projected points when no line exists). **{{ L.n_eff_models }}** regressions are fit, one per stat and position group, on as-of features from earlier seasons. The opponent enters through the Vegas total and the team's projected volume, not through our own defense ratings: with the Vegas total and opponent-adjusted player rates already in, those ratings added nothing and several came out with the wrong sign (a better defense meaning more touchdowns). Leaving them out was slightly more accurate (2021-2026, section 8).
4. *Goal-line role.* Rushing touchdowns per carry also get the expected share of the player's carries that will come inside the 10: his share of his team's inside-10 carries relative to his overall carry share (pulled toward "no special role" when his sample is small), times how often his team is expected to run inside the 10, which is learned from its implied points and run-offense rating. A goal-line back on a team that keeps reaching the goal line gains; the same role on a team that rarely gets there gains little.

**Weather.** Wind above **{{ L.wind_thr }}** mph cuts passing efficiency (pass yards and TDs, catches, receiving yards and TDs) by **{{ L.wind_pen }}** per mph, capped at **{{ L.wind_cap }}**. Domes, closed roofs and neutral sites count as calm. The live week uses the forecast; backtests and archived weeks use the measured game-day wind.

**Expert blend.** Once the FantasyPros consensus is published (Fridays), RB/WR/TE projections blend it in with learned weights. QBs stay model-only because blending made QB projections worse. Only rankings scraped before game day are used, so players in Thursday games get no Friday rank. Current blends (plus a small fitted intercept, shown in section 10.5): {{ L.blend }}.

**Ranges and touchdown odds.** The floor, middle and ceiling are the 10th / 25th / 75th / 90th percentiles of what actually happened after past projections of the same size at the position. (Extra widening for thin data or injury-driven roles was tested and removed: it made calibration worse.) Backtest coverage: **{{ L.coverage }}** of actual scores landed between floor and ceiling (target 80%). Touchdown odds (exactly 1, 2, 3+) treat the expected-TD rate as a Poisson count, which matched real frequencies closely in testing.

## 5. Availability and injuries

- **Chance to play.** Gradient-boosted trees on the Wed/Thu/Fri practice sequence (did not practice / limited / full) and game status, trained on every season from 2018 through the last training season. Example: for a Questionable player who did not practice all three days, the model gives roughly a 30% chance to play, versus roughly 85% for limited, limited, full (current values in section 10.6). Before Wednesday's report, last week's status carries forward using learned week-to-week rates.
- **Midweek, before the final report.** On Wednesday and Thursday nobody has a game status yet, and a model trained on complete weeks reads "no status" as "cleared": after one practice day it said 86% to play when 65% of those players did. So until a team's final report is out (anyone on it has a game status, or it has three practice days), a separate model for one and for two published days is used, trained on past report weeks cut to their first one or two days, with every outcome. Walk-forward 2021-25 it cut the error (Brier score) from 0.203 to 0.160, better in every season, and its average matches what happened within 1.2 points. A Wednesday DNP now reads about 40% to play (it had been about 80%); a Wednesday full practice barely moves (about 87%).
- **Early-week injury news.** A player hurt in Sunday's game is on no report until Wednesday, so he would look healthy for three days. For the current week only, while his team's report is not out, an ESPN status posted after his team's last game puts him in one of four groups, each with its own chance to play, measured on 2022-25 from archived copies of ESPN's injury page (Monday and Tuesday captures in the Wayback Machine, 23,695 listings matched to the game-day inactive lists): Out with ESPN's estimated return at least a week past this week's game, 1.8% played (12 of 652); Out otherwise, 33.5%; Doubtful, 4.2% (ESPN's early-week Doubtful is not the official one, whose carry-forward rate is about half); injured reserve, inactive. Closer return dates are mostly ESPN's next-game placeholder and play like a plain Out (1-6 days past: 28% played). ESPN's return dates run optimistic, not pessimistic: players came back a median 14 days later than estimated and almost never earlier (1%). Past seasons count as at most 50 cases, so this season's outcomes (saved daily from 2026-10-13) can still move each rate. Labeled "(ESPN)" on the site. ESPN's Questionable is ignored.
- **Midweek rulings.** Once a team's practice report is out, a player's chance to play comes from his practice days, which can't see a team ruling him out after a full practice (Jalen Hurts, 2026 week 6: full practice Wednesday, ruled out Thursday). So for a player on this week's practice report whose ESPN status, posted after his team's last game, is Out or injured reserve, and who has no final game status yet, the measured rate for that status replaces the practice model until the final report: Out with ESPN's return date at least a week past the game, 0% played (0 of 43); Out otherwise, 9.6% (of 187); injured reserve while practicing (designated to return), 38% (of 258). Measured on 2022-24 Wednesday-Friday captures of ESPN's injury page, Sunday games, players on the report: it cut the error (Brier score) from 0.419 to 0.164, better in all three seasons, and each rate is within 3 points of what happened when estimated from the other seasons. ESPN Doubtful is treated the same way at its measured rate, 2.8% (2 of 71): added on the owner's call rather than a test (too few cases for one), and every source agrees Doubtful players almost never play (official Friday Doubtful 2021-25: 3.8% of 573). Labeled "Out (ESPN)", "Doubtful (ESPN)" or "IR (ESPN)" next to his practice days.
- **Healthy scratches.** For players not on the report, a second model uses how often the player has been active this season, whether he played last week, snap share, depth-chart rank, rookie status, and how many teammates at his position ahead of him are out.
- **Scenarios, not discounts.** NFL availability is yes or no, so every projection is "if active", with Inactive% shown separately.
- **Who sits.** Players below 50% are assumed out, as are contributors no longer on the roster (injured reserve, cut, traded). Their volume moves to teammates when they are a real contributor: an RB/WR/TE with an 8%+ projected target share or 15%+ carry share who has played this season, or the starting QB.
- **"+X if he sits".** Teammates of a significant contributor with a 50-90% chance to play show this contingent bump when the gain is at least 1 point.
- **Redistribution.** Vacated targets and carries go where they actually went in past absences, learned by position pair, depth-chart rank and current share. It is corrected so a player who has already missed several games is not double-counted, and currently applied at **{{ L.redist_scale }}** strength. When a starting QB sits, the backup inherits his dropbacks (and scrambles at his own rate), and the whole passing offense uses the backup's quarterback rating.

## 6. What the report numbers mean

| Number | Definition |
|---|---|
| Matchup | Points the opponent adds (+) or takes away (-): his projection minus the same projection against a league-average opponent, with his role, team, home field and weather unchanged. Two channels. The Vegas total: every team total this season is split into offense + opponent defense + home field (least squares, earlier weeks and last season weighted down like the team ratings), and the opponent's defense term comes out. Team volume: the game model's Vegas volume formulas re-run with the lines he'd get against an average opponent. Green helps, red hurts, white = 0.0; the color deepens with size, measured as how far the matchup moves his median within his own range of outcomes, full color at the week's 90th percentile across fantasy starters (top 12 QB / 24 RB / 36 WR / 12 TE, pooled, so small WR effects stay pale). "Soft / Tough matchup" tags: the week's top quarter. Half-PPR; not re-scored by the scoring panel |
| Battle bars (gamecast Pregame tab) | Tug-of-war bars in EPA per 100 plays, with the number on the side that wins the battle ("even" under 0.5). Each offense vs the other defense: offense rating (including its projected starting QB) minus the defense's, the total weighting passing and running by that offense's projected dropbacks and runs (tap for Pass/Run). Special teams: the two units' net rating per 100 special-teams plays. Home field: the home edge per scrimmage play. Bar length = the edge's impact on the game through the points formula, on one scale (section 3). Pace appears as projected plays and pass rate. No points and no overall margin are shown; rank chips come from the Power tab |
| Win chances (Matchups tiles, gamecast Pregame tab) | Headline: the market's chance from the Vegas spread, or ours before lines post; under {{ L.tossup }}% it is a tossup. "Our model" ("ours" on tiles): Power-rank gap + Elo + home field (section 3). On the Pregame tab the bar splits at the away team's chance in the two teams' colors; its shaded middle is the tossup zone. The spread shown is always Vegas's. Track record (end of this page): how often the market's favorites won at each confidence level, each season scored with a curve fit only on earlier seasons |
| Matchups tiles | One tile per game (one per row on phones, as many as fit on wider screens); tapping it opens the gamecast. A big number on each team's side: before kickoff its win chance (the market's, or ours before a line posts; the underdog dimmed, both dimmed for a tossup), with the time, the Vegas spread and our model's chance underneath; once the game starts, the score, with the clock, possession and down and distance (from ESPN); after it, the final score and how the forecast fared (favorite won, upset at its chance, or a tossup) |
| Lookahead weeks (picker) | Later weeks of the season projected from today's data against that week's real opponents. Availability comes from the return-timing model: for a player out injured, the chance he plays his team's next game is learned from every injury absence in the loaded seasons, including this season's resolved ones (games already missed, roster designation such as injured reserve or PUP, injury-report status, the injury, position, week), then chained game by game, with players who return staying active at the measured rate. Walk-forward 2021-25 it beats a designation x games-missed table (Brier 0.132 vs 0.137). Under 50% he sits and his volume is redistributed; injured-reserve players expected back are added to the roster. Injury reports list body parts, not diagnoses, so severity is only inferred from the designation and time missed; ESPN's diagnosis and return estimates are being archived daily to test as an input. Everyone else is assumed healthy (no healthy-scratch model), weather is neutral, and there is no expert blend. Vegas lines are used once posted. Ratings and usage are weighted for the target week, so last season's plays fade by the target week, not today's |
| Player card (tap a player) | The card is the player's, not the page's: his photo, jersey number, age, height, weight, years in the NFL, snap share in his last 3 games (and for quarterbacks how often he scrambles, this season and since 2019), name, team, game log and news are always current, and a week picker (any week since 2021 plus upcoming ones; starts on the page's week) chooses which week the Week tab shows. Players we didn't list that week still get a card saying so, with his actual line if he played. Everything on one player for the picked week: projection and range under your scoring, expected line and volume (targets, carries, attempts), matchup, chance to be inactive and why, season totals and usage, points by week, and his live or final line once his game starts. ## 8. Tested and left out

**Full audit (2026-10-12).** Every setting that had been hand-set, plus everything earlier decided on only one or two seasons, was re-tested walk-forward on 2021-2025 (12,480 fantasy-relevant player-weeks; 95% confidence intervals over season-weeks):

- *Changed:* usage-share half-life 5 -> 3 weeks (better, interval excludes zero); range-widening multipliers removed (calibration 78.3% / 49.5% -> 80.0% / 50.2% vs targets 80 / 50); small-rate priors (scramble rate, fumbles, aDOT, targets per route) now estimated from data.
- *Confirmed (alternatives worse):* Vegas implied total (+0.036 without), expert blend for RB/WR/TE (+0.013 without) but not for QBs (blending hurt), a fixed-shape wind penalty (no wind or a fitted wind term both hurt TEs), half-strength injury redistribution, the 0.96 starting-QB share.
- *Insensitive for fantasy projections (alternatives within noise, current kept):* training window (2 / 3 / 4 seasons), prior-season weight and fade, efficiency season weights, garbage-time window, QB-experience cutoff and penalty, touchdown blend strength, availability-model size, redistribution trigger, team-rating half-life. (Prior-season carryover does matter for game outcomes; see below.)
- *Usage shares:* each team's projected carries and targets are now pulled toward the totals a real game holds (carries 70% of the way, targets 30%; strengths chosen by backtest). Shares are predicted player by player, so team totals used to swing more than reality, over-projecting players on crowded backfields. Overall error -0.017, RB -0.043, RB ranking accuracy .507 -> .525.
- *Follow-up rounds:* wind penalty grid-tuned to 10 mph / 3% per mph / 25% cap (better than the old 15 mph / 2%). No difference: position-specific share half-lives, game-script (spread-aware) shares, finer draft-pick tiers. Rejected: a final linear calibration of projections (worse on average error).
- *Game model:* the points formula's special-teams weight drifted 0.9-3.3 when fit on two seasons; it now trains on six (weight about 2; margin error 10.71 -> 10.68, never worse in any season, winners 59% -> 61%). Fixed special-teams weights from 0 to 2.5 were all within noise of the fit.

**Game outcomes (2026-10-12),** walk-forward on 2022-26:

- **Win chances.** The market's win chances beat the model's, and no blend with the model helped. Against the spread, the model's lean covered 51.7% (break-even 52.4%), and bigger model-vs-Vegas disagreements did worse. Margin corrections (flat recalibration, team "mistake memory") did not help.
- **Power rank.** It beat the rating gap it comes from and was adopted.
- **28 pre-game signals.** Only Elo helped, and it shipped as part of our win chance. The rest did nothing: QB tiers, record, point differential, close-game luck, turnovers, streaks, rest, byes, travel, prime time, division games, cold weather, surface, new coaches, referees.
- **Referees.** No referee or referee-team effect on spread or total results persists from one set of games to the next.
- **Prior-season carryover in team ratings.** A quick three-season check found more of last season helps weeks 2-8 but hurts later weeks. Not adopted; parked for a fuller test.

**Owner ideas (2026-10-13),** walk-forward on 2021-2025, switched off (config `[experiments]`):

- **Our defense ratings as a player-efficiency input.** Each efficiency regression used to take the opponent's pass or run defense rating as well as the Vegas total. With the Vegas total and opponent-adjusted player rates in, those terms fit near zero and several had the wrong sign. Walk-forward 2021-2026, average error: leaving them out -0.002 points [-0.004, -0.000] (quarterbacks -0.008 [-0.016, -0.000], the other positions no difference, better in all 6 seasons) and no difference in any touchdown chance; keeping them with the sign forced right made no difference. So they are out. Dropping the Vegas total instead was clearly worse at every position (+0.029 [+0.020, +0.038]): the market carries the opponent information.
- **Weighting plays against elite opposing units.** Plays facing a top-ranked opposing *unit* (top 3, 5, 8 or 12 by that week's ratings, or a smooth ramp by rank) were given extra weight in the team ratings, 1.25x to 2x. Judged on how well the ratings predict each team-game's efficiency the following week: no setting helped, and doubling the weight against top-5 or top-8 units made predictions measurably worse (pass and run). Player projections were unchanged. The ratings already adjust every play for opponent strength, and the few elite matchups add more noise than information.
- **Running-back committees.** Each team's lead-back share of RB carries (about 0.54 for the most committee-heavy teams to 0.87 for bellcow teams in 2025) is a moderate team trait (season-to-season correlation 0.36; a returning head coach did not make it reliably stickier). As an input to carry-share projections it changed RB error by -0.001 points (95% interval -0.003 to +0.001): a back's own recent share already carries the information.
- **Goal-line share.** A back's share of his team's inside-the-10 carries, relative to his overall carry share, as an input to touchdowns per carry: RB error -0.002 (-0.005 to +0.001); together with committee tendency -0.003 (-0.006 to +0.000). Touchdown expectations are already built from where each carry happened, which captures most of a goal-line role. Not adopted under the rule that the interval must exclude zero. A follow-up scored it directly on the touchdown odds the site shows (chance of 1+ rushing TD, 3,840 RB-weeks): Brier 0.1896 -> 0.1893, difference -0.0003 (-0.0007 to +0.0001), still no difference overall. It does help backs whose goal-line share runs well above their carry share (actual 34.9%, now 30.8%, with it 32.5%), but over-corrects the opposite group, so the total is a wash.
- **Goal-line role x team goal-line volume: adopted** (section 4, step 4). The miss above came from teams that reach the goal line a lot: goal-line backs on the highest-scoring third of teams scored a rushing TD 47% of the time against 38% predicted, while on the lowest-scoring third the prediction was already right. Scaling the role by the team's expected goal-line carries, tested with criteria set in advance: chance of 1+ rushing TD Brier 0.1896 -> 0.1894, difference -0.00021 (-0.00043 to -0.000004), better in 4 of 5 seasons, touchdown counts also better predicted, fantasy-point error not worse (-0.0004, -0.0018 to +0.0009). A small but real gain; most of the high-scoring-team gap (8.8 -> 7.6 points) remains.
- **Quarterbacks who vulture goal-line runs.** Backs whose QB takes many inside-10 runs are not over-projected, because a back's goal-line share is measured against all of his team's goal-line runs, QB sneaks included. The real miss is the other kind of QB: pocket passers on high-scoring teams got too much rushing-touchdown credit (about 9% actual vs 13% predicted). Splitting QB runs into scrambles and designed runs, with team scoring boosting designed runs only, narrowed that gap but not enough to register: chance of 1+ rushing TD Brier -0.0002 (-0.0006 to +0.0002), QB points error +0.001 (-0.003 to +0.006). Left off.
- **Ranges for committee backs.** Range calibration was checked for RBs by team committee tendency: every group is on target within its interval (10-90 range: committee 78.4%, middle 81.3%, bellcow 78.9% vs 80%; 25-75 range 47.5% / 51.8% / 48.9% vs 50%). No change needed.

**Charting stats (owner, 2026-10-14), tested and left out.** Skill beyond a player's raw rates, from nflverse's completion-probability and expected-YAC models: catches over expected per target (feeding receptions), yards after catch over expected (receiving yards), completions over expected (quarterback passing yards), each shrunk toward the league like the other small rates. Walk-forward 2021-2026 (12,948 player-weeks), criteria set in advance: every variant and the combination changed average error by at most 0.001 points at any position, every interval spanning zero. A player's own recent yards and catch rates already carry it. Team pass rate over expected was already in the volume model. Participation charting (personnel, coverage, pressure) is not published for the current season, and FTN charting starts in 2022, too few seasons to test.

Ideas that sounded right but did not improve out-of-sample accuracy are switched off, not shipped:

- **Team-specific home field.** Team home edges do not persist year to year (correlation -0.02 over 567 team-seasons from 2005-25; Seattle's edge was +5.7 points in 2005-12 and about zero since 2019). Travel distance and time zones showed no effect. A division-game term made cross-validation worse.
- **Defensive and offensive-line absences.** Stat-weighted defender values overfit. Counting only absences knowable before kickoff, no position group (cornerbacks included) or offensive line showed a reliable effect beyond the team rating.
- **Air-yards share, and snap share as an input.** No consistent gain (snap share stays as a role-change alert).
- **Route participation.** Not published for the current season.
- **Time of possession / pace and drive sustainability in the volume model.** Neutral-situation seconds per play and expected EPA per play (offense vs this defense, both ways) did not improve out-of-sample team play counts (plays MAE 6.88 -> 6.87-6.92 on 2025, 6.75 -> 6.78-6.80 on 2026). Drive outcomes are already inside EPA, and time of possession mostly reflects game script, which the Vegas spread captures.

## 9. Keeping this page current

This page is generated from `MODEL.md` in the repository, and the bold values are filled in live at every build. The document carries a fingerprint of the projection and rating code and the model settings in the configuration. Presentation code (the report builder, this page's generator, the page template) and the report and tier settings, including the win-chance constants, are not covered, so changes there rely on review. If covered code or settings change without the document being reviewed:

- **On commit:** the git pre-commit hook stops the commit.
- **On build:** GitHub flags a warning.
- **On the page:** this tab shows an "out of date" notice (current-week page).

After updating the text, run `python tools/model_doc.py --stamp` to record the new fingerprint.
