# How the model works

## The short version

NFL Edge makes three kinds of estimates each week: how many fantasy points every quarterback, running back, receiver and tight end will score; how strong every team is; and each team's chance to win its game. Everything here is an estimate with honest error bars, not a pick or a betting tip.

**Grading every play.** Football is made of plays, so we start there. Each play is graded against what normally happens in that exact spot: a 6-yard gain on 3rd-and-5 is great, the same 6 yards on 3rd-and-10 is a failure. Credit and blame are split between the offense and the defense on the field, and adjusted for who they faced, so shredding a great defense counts for more than shredding a bad one. Recent games count most, and last season's play fades out as this one goes on. The result is a rating for every team's passing offense, running offense, pass defense, run defense and special teams. Those ratings are the **Power** rankings, shown as points per game better or worse than an average team.

**Predicting a game.** From those ratings, plus each team's usual pace, we estimate how many plays each team will run, how often it will throw, and how well it should move the ball against this particular opponent.

**Predicting a player.** A player's projection is three things multiplied together:

- *How much of the work he gets:* his share of his team's passes, carries or targets, weighted toward recent games.
- *How much the team will have:* the plays from the game prediction above.
- *How productive he is per chance:* yards and touchdowns per target or carry, adjusted for how many points Vegas expects his team to score against this opponent, the teammates around him, and strong wind. A back who gets the goal-line carries gets a touchdown boost, but only as big as his team's chances to get near the goal line.

A player with only a few games is pulled toward what is typical for players like him (same position, similar draft slot), so two hot weeks aren't treated as the new normal. As the season goes on, his own numbers take over.

**Injuries.** Practice reports (did not practice / limited / full) and game status turn into a chance he plays. If he is likely out, his share goes to teammates the way it has gone in past absences. A healthy player's risk of being left off the game-day roster (a healthy scratch) is a separate number. Projections are always "if he plays", with his chance to play shown next to it.

**Experts.** Once the weekly expert rankings come out, they are mixed in for running backs, receivers and tight ends, because testing showed the mix beats either alone. Quarterbacks stay ours alone, because the mix made them worse.


**Ranges.** Instead of one number you also get a floor and a ceiling, based on how real scores have spread around past projections like this one. About 8 times in 10 the actual score lands between them.

**Win chances.** The headline chance is the betting market's, turned into a percentage, because over years of games it has been the most accurate forecast. Next to it is our own independent opinion (team ratings, a score-based team rating, and home field) as a second look. Under {{ L.tossup }}% we call it a tossup. A 70% favorite should still lose about 3 times in 10, so an upset isn't automatically a miss.

**Why trust it.** Every part was tested on past seasons the way it would have worked then, using only information available before each game. A change only stays if that testing shows a clear improvement. The detailed sections below describe every piece, with the live formulas at the end.

**How we test.** Every change to the model is tested on past seasons, with each season predicted only from the seasons before it. The bar a change must clear is written down before anyone sees the results, and failures are kept on record, not quietly dropped. Each area below ends with "What we tried": every idea tested for that area, shipped or rejected, with the key number. The full raw lab notebook is at [docs/INSIGHTS.md](https://github.com/ChefJulio/nfl-edge/blob/main/docs/INSIGHTS.md).

## Site guide

Every page and tab on NFL Edge: what it shows, where its numbers come from, and what it is for. The model behind the numbers is described in the other areas of this tab; this one is the map.

### Getting around

- **Five tabs**: Players, Teams, Games, Fantasy, Model. On phones they sit in a bar at the bottom with each tab's sub-tabs above it; on wider screens they run across the top. A tab reopens the sub-tab you last used there.
- **The week picker** (top of every page) moves between weeks: the arrows step one week, the dropdown jumps to any week since 2021. The page is labeled This week, Past week or Upcoming, and keeps the tab you were on. Past weeks show what we projected before kickoff (built only from what was known then) next to what happened. This season's past weeks are rebuilt with the current model whenever it changes, so Power's arrows and trend on this week's page match what those pages show. Upcoming weeks have no injury report or weather yet.
- **The (i) button** next to the picker says which games the projections use, how many of this week's games are final, the scoring, how many teams' final injury reports are in and when the next is due, and when the page was last updated. It turns amber while reports are still coming.
- **Player and team cards** open over whatever you are looking at (tap any player or team anywhere on the site). Cards can stack, swipe down to close on phones, and every card has its own link you can share.
- **Updates**: the site rebuilds several times a day (every morning, after each day's practice reports, Saturday, before each kickoff window once the inactives are posted, and when a key player's injury status changes), and a "Site updated · Refresh" note appears when a newer version is out. Scores, plays and live points come straight from ESPN while games are on: every 30 seconds on the week pages (20 with the Fantasy live feed open), every 15 seconds on an open game page.
- **Your scoring**: the Scoring & league panel on Rankings sets the scoring for the whole site (Rankings, player cards, game pages, Stats). A connected fantasy league uses its own scoring on the Fantasy tab and game pages.

### Players

**Rankings**: who to start this week, and who holds value the rest of the way.

- Weekly or Rest of season; positions QB, RB, WR, TE, FLEX (the top 150 RB / WR / TE together), K and D/ST; a name search.
- Weekly columns:
  - **#**: our position rank, colored by your league size (green = a starter, amber = flex range, gray = bench).
  - **Player**: his injury tag and chance to play when there's any doubt, his healthy-scratch risk ("Scratch 30%"), and any boost from a teammate's absence ("+2.1 inj").
  - **Pts**: our projection (hover for his floor, middle and ceiling). A player under 10% to play projects 0.0 with "if he plays" beneath; "+1.2 if Moore sits" lines show how a teammate's status would change him. Once his game kicks off this becomes his actual points, marked live or final, with the projection under it: green above his likely range, red below it.
  - **Matchup**: what this opponent adds or takes away versus an average one, with a weather mark for strong wind, rain or snow.
  - **Grd**: his Edge grade. **Last 5**: his last five games as bars against our projections. **Snap%**: his share of the offense's snaps. **TD**: his chance to score (passing TDs for QBs).
- Rest of season: points expected from now through week 17, each game weighted by his chance to play, with per-game points, expected games, schedule, a week-by-week strip, and his season pace.
- Kickers and team defenses use standard ESPN scoring here; the panel's settings apply to QB, RB, WR and TE.
- **Scoring & league panel**: presets (Half PPR, PPR, Standard, 6-pt pass TD, TE premium), every per-stat value, the defenders' (IDP) values, and your league's size and starters (which set the tier colors). Saved on your device.

**Stats**: what a player actually did, in any season or career, in your scoring.

- Seasons from 1999 (regular season or playoffs), All years (career totals), every position group, totals or per game.
- Each position has its own default columns, including fantasy points; the last three columns are yours to pick from about 90 stats (bio, draft, contract, usage, efficiency, defense, pass rush, coverage, tackling, kicking, punting, returns, fantasy).
- Search finds any player in any season: typing a name searches every season and position at once (career totals, active players first, then the longest careers); a team code ("dal") filters the season you picked.
- Defenders' pressures, coverage and missed tackles come from Pro Football Reference (2018 on); snaps from 2012.

**Roles**: who just got a bigger job. Players whose snap share or share of the offense's touches jumped last week versus their previous three games, with a plain-English takeaway (waiver add, or watch).

**Injuries**: is he playing, and who do I pivot to?

- This week: everyone on our board with an injury status, grouped as game-time calls (15-85% to play), expected out, and probably playing, with our chance to play, projection and kickoff.
- Full list: ESPN's whole injury list by team and status (questionable, out, IR), with our chance to play, ESPN's estimated return and its latest note.

**Moves**: every signing, release, practice-squad move, injury-list move and trade from ESPN's transactions log since 2002, filterable by team, type, position and date.

**Trends**: a situation explorer over every play since 1999. Build a sentence from tappable pieces (a player, team or the whole league; a stat such as yards per carry, EPA, success rate, completion over expected or pressure rate; filters such as down, distance, field position, score, quarter, personnel or coverage; grouping by player, team, season and more) and get a leaderboard against the league baseline, or a trend over seasons. Charting-based filters only exist for the seasons that have the charting, and the page says which seasons it left out. Every query has a shareable link.

### Teams

**All teams**: all 32 teams by division (sorted by record), with record and Power rank. Tap one for its team card.

**Power**: how good each team is from here on, in points per game against an average team, built from every play (passing, running and special teams rated separately, adjusted for opponents, recent weeks counting more). Offense, defense and each unit get a rank (green top 8, red bottom 8); a whisker shows how sure the rank is; arrows show the move since last week's page; a sparkline shows the season (past weeks as their pages show them), with hollow rings on weeks with a backup quarterback. An injured starting quarterback counts in each remaining game by his chance to be back.

**Standings**: every season since 2002, by division or as the playoff picture (seeds, with a line at the cut). The tiebreaker that decided an order is named under the team.

**Playoffs**: this season's odds and every postseason since 1920.

- Odds: 10,000 simulated seasons as of this week (Vegas lines where posted, our model otherwise, real tiebreakers and playoff format): the most likely seeding, and every team's chance to make it, win the division, get the bye, reach each round and win the Super Bowl, with a track record of how those odds have held up.
- Bracket: a projected bracket before the playoffs, then the real one; past seasons' brackets by era, games linking to their game pages from 1999.
- Each team's odds by week and its rooting guide live on its team card's Playoffs tab.

**Stats**: team offense and defense since 1999 from the play-by-play: points, yards, efficiency, turnovers, third and fourth downs, red zone, EPA per play and success rate, totals or per game.

**Draft**: every pick since 1980 with how the career turned out (games, seasons as a starter, Pro Bowls, All-Pro, Hall of Fame, Pro Football Reference's Approximate Value), filterable by team, position, round, player or school.

### Games

**Matchups**: this week's games as tiles. Before kickoff each side shows its win chance (from the Vegas line, or our model before a line posts, with our model's own number underneath); during the game the score, clock, possession and red zone, live from ESPN; after it the final and how the favorite fared. Live games sort to the top. On phones a compact grid fits the whole week on one screen, grouped by kickoff window. The games with the most playoff odds riding on them are flagged. Tap a tile for its game page.

**The game page (gamecast)**: everything about one game, two tabs.

- **Pregame** (ours, made before kickoff): win chances (Vegas and our model), who wins each battle (each offense against the other defense, passing and running, special teams and home field, in points), projected plays and pass rate, team ratings, every fantasy player's projection, range and matchup, and both depth charts.
- **Live** (labeled Final after the game), from ESPN: the Now card (down and distance, a field drawing with the current drive play by play, the last play with what it was worth to each fantasy player, and ESPN's live win chance); between plays it shows what's next (the try, the kickoff, halftime). Under it four panes:
  - Game flow: the win-chance chart for the whole game (tap or drag for any moment), drive chips by half (tap a drive for its own sheet: every play, a field drawing, the win-chance swing and the fantasy points it produced), and after the game the biggest turning points.
  - Plays: Recent (the current and previous drive, newest first, with an "Awaiting result" line before the next snap) and Game log (every play by drive or one list, sortable by the biggest swings, filters for scoring, turnovers and penalties, a search). Each play shows down and distance and the ball's spot before and after.
  - Box score: each team's full box, and Fantasy (both teams side by side by position, kickers and defenses included, each player's points against his projection, your players marked if a league is connected).
  - Team stats: yards, downs, turnovers, possession and the rest, side by side.
- **Replay**: a finished game can be replayed play by play (play, pause, step, three speeds, a slider); the score, field, drive, win chance and box score are rebuilt as of each play.

### Fantasy

Connect a Sleeper league (your username, then pick the league) or a public ESPN league (its league id); the league, your team and its scoring are saved on this device, nothing goes to us. Your league's own scoring is imported (offense, kickers, team defenses, defenders), and anything we can't model (yardage bonuses, two-point tries and the like) is listed in the header.

- **Lineup**: your best lineup by expected points (our projection in your scoring times his chance to play), what to start and bench, game-time calls with what to do if he plays or sits, IR players and when they're likely back, and the free agents who would improve your team.
- **Matchup**: both lineups slot by slot and your win chance, which counts each player's range, his chance to sit, and how players in the same game rise and fall together.
- **Live**: every play this week that moved your starters' points (and your opponent's), scored your way, newest first.
- **Teams**: every team in the league ranked by its best lineups over the rest of the season.
- **Waivers**: the best available players by position, and how much each one adds to your team per week after your weakest drop.
- **Trade**: pick players to give and get and see what it does to both teams for the rest of the season (each player valued over the best free agent at his position), or let it find trades that help both sides.

### Model

**Model** (this page): how every number is made, what was tested and kept or rejected, and track records for the chance to play and for how often favorites win. A note at the top warns if the description may be behind the code.

**Reference**: how often things happen in the NFL, from every regular-season play since 2001: conversions by down and distance, fourth downs, short yardage, turnovers, explosive plays, pass rate, touchdown chance by yard line, drives by starting spot, expected points, win chance by score and time, how often leads hold, field goals by distance, punts, kicks, penalties and replay. Every table says what a cell is; the window picker chooses all seasons, the last ten or three, or since the relevant rule change. History, not our model.

## Projections

Each player's projection is "if he plays": his share of the team's work, times the team's expected plays, times his production per chance.

### How a projection is built

**Projection = projected volume × efficiency per opportunity**, computed stat by stat (passing yards, TDs and interceptions; rushing yards and TDs; catches, receiving yards and TDs; fumbles) and then scored with the scoring file (default half-PPR).

**Volume.** Usage shares are taken over games the player actually played: dropbacks for QBs, share of designed carries, and targets per team dropback.

- **Recency.** This season's games are weighted with a half-life of **{{ L.hl_share }}** weeks. Last season's games carry the same flat, fading weight as in the team ratings.
- **Regression to the mean.** A learned calibration pulls each share toward the player's longer-run average, because recent spikes partly regress.
- **Team totals.** Each team's projected carries and targets are then pulled toward the totals a real game holds: carries 70% of the way, targets 30%.
- **Quarterbacks.** The depth-chart starter gets at least 96% of dropbacks. QB rushes = designed carries + scramble rate x dropbacks.

The team's projected dropbacks and rushes then turn shares into opportunities.

**Efficiency.** For each stat per opportunity (yards per target, catch rate, TD rate and so on):

1. *Empirical-Bayes shrinkage.* The player's rate, over this season and the two before (weighted 1 / 0.6 / 0.3), is pulled toward the average for his position and draft tier (round 1, rounds 2-3, rounds 4-7, undrafted; a tier with too little data falls back to the position average). How hard depends on how noisy that stat is, estimated from data as a "prior strength" k in pseudo-opportunities. Examples: WR catch rate k ≈ **{{ L.k_wr_catch }}**, WR yards per target k ≈ **{{ L.k_wr_ypt }}**, RB yards per carry k ≈ **{{ L.k_rb_ypc }}**. A small k means the stat is mostly real skill; a large k means it is mostly noise.
2. *Expected touchdowns.* Instead of raw TDs, each touch and target carries the historical TD rate for its field position (and end-zone targets). The player's TD rate blends toward his actual TDs as his sample grows (weight n / (n + **{{ L.td_k }}**)).
3. *Learned efficiency regressions* combine the shrunk rate; the player's opponent-adjusted EPA; his own unit's offensive rating, i.e. the supporting cast (pass offense with the projected starting QB for passing and receiving stats, run offense for rushing); home field; and the Vegas implied team total (the model's own projected points when no line exists). **{{ L.n_eff_models }}** regressions are fit, one per stat and position group, on as-of features from earlier seasons. The opponent enters through the Vegas total and the team's projected volume, not through our own defense ratings: with the Vegas total and opponent-adjusted player rates already in, those ratings added nothing and several came out with the wrong sign (a better defense meaning more touchdowns). Leaving them out was slightly more accurate (2021-2026; see What we tried below).
4. *Goal-line role.* Rushing touchdowns per carry also get the expected share of the player's carries that will come inside the 10: his share of his team's inside-10 carries relative to his overall carry share (pulled toward "no special role" when his sample is small), times how often his team is expected to run inside the 10, which is learned from its implied points and run-offense rating. A goal-line back on a team that keeps reaching the goal line gains; the same role on a team that rarely gets there gains little.

**Weather.** Wind above **{{ L.wind_thr }}** mph cuts passing efficiency (pass yards and TDs, catches, receiving yards and TDs) by **{{ L.wind_pen }}** per mph, capped at **{{ L.wind_cap }}**. Domes, closed roofs and neutral sites count as calm. The live week uses the forecast; backtests and archived weeks use the measured game-day wind.

**Expert blend.** Once the FantasyPros consensus is published (Fridays), RB/WR/TE projections blend it in with learned weights. QBs stay model-only because blending made QB projections worse. Only rankings scraped before game day are used, so players in Thursday games get no Friday rank. Current blends (plus a small fitted intercept, shown in formula 10.5 under Under the hood): {{ L.blend }}.

### Ranges and touchdown odds

**Ranges and touchdown odds.** The floor, middle and ceiling are the 10th / 25th / 75th / 90th percentiles of what actually happened after past projections of the same size at the position. (Extra widening for thin data or injury-driven roles was tested and removed: it made calibration worse.) Backtest coverage: **{{ L.coverage }}** of actual scores landed between floor and ceiling (target 80%). Touchdown odds (exactly 1, 2, 3+) treat the expected-TD rate as a Poisson count, which matched real frequencies closely in testing.

### Newcomers

- **New arrivals.** A running back or receiver in his first game for a team he has never played for gets half the targets and carries his past role suggests, and his new teammates at the position get the rest: players who just changed teams scored about 40% of their projection in that first game (2021-25), then were back to normal by the second. Walk-forward it improved those players and their teammates (average miss -0.009 points) without changing anyone else.
- **Journeymen.** Players who change teams often come in under their projections, more the more they move. Over his past five seasons plus this one, one team change scales his whole projection by 0.94, two by 0.91, three or more by 0.89. Fit on 2021-23 and tested on 2024-25, it cut the average miss for those players by 0.11 points and for everyone by 0.04, better at running back, receiver and tight end (no difference at quarterback). Idea from the owner.
- **Debuts.** A running back, receiver or tight end with no games in the data (a rookie's first game) used to get no usage at all, so first-round rookies projected near zero in week 1 (Bijan Robinson 0.0 in his 2023 debut; he scored 17.3). If the depth chart lists him first or second at his position, he now gets the target and carry shares earlier rookies drew in their first games, by depth slot and draft round; deeper rookies stay at zero, since most of them barely play. Tested on weeks 1-2 of 2016-2020, seasons no other test had used: the average miss for debuting players fell by 1.0 point and for everyone in those weeks by 0.07, better in all five seasons; later weeks unchanged.

### When a teammate sits

- **Two worlds for a backup.** Playing is yes or no, so for a teammate of an uncertain player (5-95% to sit) the site shows two numbers instead of a blend: his projection if that player plays and if he sits (the likelier one is the main projection; the other is shown under it, for up to two such teammates, biggest swing first). The Fantasy tab turns that into a game-time call: if the starter is active, start him; if he's out, start his backup (at his 'starter out' number) or whoever is better.
- **Who sits.** Players below 50% to take the field (injury, healthy-scratch risk and the backup-quarterback rule together; see Chance to play) are assumed out, as are contributors no longer on the roster (injured reserve, cut, traded). Their volume moves to teammates when they are a real contributor: an RB/WR/TE with an 8%+ projected target share or 15%+ carry share who has played this season, or the starting QB.
- **"+X if he sits".** Teammates of a significant contributor with a 50-90% chance to play show this contingent bump when the gain is at least 1 point.
- **Redistribution.** Vacated targets and carries go where they actually went in past absences, learned by position pair, depth-chart rank and current share. It is corrected so a player who has already missed several games is not double-counted, and currently applied at **{{ L.redist_scale }}** strength. When a starting QB sits, the backup inherits his dropbacks (and scrambles at his own rate), and the whole passing offense uses the backup's quarterback rating. The "+X from injuries" tag on a player is the part of his final projection that comes from teammates' absences: measured when injuries are applied, then scaled with every later adjustment so it never exceeds the projection.

### What we tried

## Chance to play

The chance a player plays this week as far as injuries go; it is shown next to the projection ("62% to play"), never folded into it. The site shows it this one way everywhere, never as a chance to sit. A healthy player's risk of being a game-day scratch is its own number (see Healthy scratches below).

### Tuesday: before the first report

- **Before the week's first injury report (Tuesday).** Until his team's first report of the week, a player who was on last week's report, or got hurt in the last game while not on it, gets his chance to play from the Tuesday model: last week's game status and practice days, whether he played the last game and his snap share there against his usual (left early), an in-game injury note from the play-by-play, games missed in a row, how long he has been on the report and every practice day since the injury first appeared, the listed reason, an extra rest week, position and age. Before this it was one number per last week's status, and a player hurt Sunday who wasn't on the report showed as healthy. Walk-forward 2021-25: for players on last week's report the error fell from 0.138 to 0.124 (Brier), better every season; for players hurt in the last game while not on its report from 0.203 to 0.153 (predicted 74.6%, played 75.0%). ESPN injury news since his last game still takes precedence where it applies.
- **Early-week injury news.** A player hurt in Sunday's game is on no report until Wednesday, so he would look healthy for three days. For the current week only, while his team's report is not out, an ESPN status posted after his team's last game puts him in one of four groups, each with its own chance to play, measured on 2022-25 from archived copies of ESPN's injury page (Monday and Tuesday captures in the Wayback Machine, 23,695 listings matched to the game-day inactive lists): Out with ESPN's estimated return at least a week past this week's game, 1.8% played (12 of 652); Out otherwise, 33.5%; Doubtful, 4.2% (ESPN's early-week Doubtful is not the official one, whose carry-forward rate is about half); injured reserve, inactive. Closer return dates are mostly ESPN's next-game placeholder and play like a plain Out (1-6 days past: 28% played). ESPN's return dates run optimistic, not pessimistic: players came back a median 14 days later than estimated and almost never earlier (1%). Past seasons count as at most 50 cases, so this season's outcomes (saved daily from 2026-10-13) can still move each rate. Labeled "(ESPN)" on the site. A team on its bye gets the same treatment for its next game (owner's rule: otherwise a player hurt the game before a bye read healthy all bye week). ESPN's Questionable is shown as a tag but never changes the chance to play: on 2022-24 midweek data it added nothing beyond the practice days (63% played vs 58% from practice alone).

### Wednesday on: one model for every day

- **From Wednesday on: one availability model for every day.** Once his team's report is out, the chance to play comes from one model that sees everything the Tuesday model does (his last game, snaps against his usual, in-game injury notes, the whole injury episode, the listed reason) plus this week's report as it stands: practice days so far, and the game status once the final report is out. Before this, separate practice models started over each week from the practice days alone, so a player who missed last week looked like any other limited practicer. Calibrated per stage of the week on the season before. Walk-forward 2021-25 (Brier, lower is better): Wednesday 0.217 -> 0.135, Thursday 0.149 -> 0.104, final report 0.095 -> 0.084, better every season and on this season's games so far; players who missed their last game: predicted 80% -> 49% on Wednesday, 48% actually played. Game status Out stays 0%.
- **Midweek rulings.** Once a team's practice report is out, a player's chance to play comes from his practice days, which can't see a team ruling him out after a full practice (Jalen Hurts, 2026 week 6: full practice Wednesday, ruled out Thursday). So for a player on this week's practice report whose ESPN status, posted after his team's last game, is Out or injured reserve, and who has no final game status yet, the measured rate for that status replaces the practice model until the final report: Out with ESPN's return date at least a week past the game, 0% played (0 of 43); Out otherwise, 9.6% (of 187); injured reserve while practicing (designated to return), 38% (of 258). Measured on 2022-24 Wednesday-Friday captures of ESPN's injury page, Sunday games, players on the report: it cut the error (Brier score) from 0.419 to 0.164, better in all three seasons, and each rate is within 3 points of what happened when estimated from the other seasons. ESPN Doubtful is treated the same way at its measured rate, 2.8% (2 of 71): added on the owner's call rather than a test (too few cases for one), and every source agrees Doubtful players almost never play (official Friday Doubtful 2021-25: 3.8% of 573). Labeled "Out (ESPN)", "Doubtful (ESPN)" or "IR (ESPN)" next to his practice days.
- **Game-day rulings.** Teams post their inactive lists 90 minutes before kickoff, and ESPN marks them minutes later. Once a team is past that point, ESPN's game-day rulings set the chance to play at measured rates (2019-25 running backs, receivers, tight ends and quarterbacks): declared inactive, 0% (no declared inactive played); Questionable and declared active, 97.4% (of 1,992); Doubtful and declared active, 28.6% (of 14, few cases). Everyone else keeps the model's chance. Labeled "Inactive" or "Active" next to his practice days. A ruling is a fact about the week, so it keeps holding after his game ends for the rest of the week (each ruling is saved when first seen, because ESPN's entry moves on after the game and nflverse's rosters mark the inactives only a day or two later). Each ruling is archived with the model's chance before it, to check the rule on this season's games.

### The earlier practice models (now the fallback)

These were the chance to play before the one model above; they stay as its fallback for a player it can't score.

- **Chance to play.** Gradient-boosted trees on the Wed/Thu/Fri practice sequence (did not practice / limited / full) and game status, trained on every season from 2018 through the last training season. Example: for a Questionable player who did not practice all three days, the model gives roughly a 30% chance to play, versus roughly 85% for limited, limited, full (current values in formula 10.6 under Under the hood). Before Wednesday's report, last week's status carries forward using learned week-to-week rates.
- **Role on the injury report.** For running backs, receivers and tight ends, the chance to play from practice reports also knows the player's recent role (his snap share over his last three games): a starter listed with full practice plays far more often than a backup with the same report (97% vs much less). Quarterbacks get no role input, which the test showed works better for them. Walk-forward 2021-25: the error of those chances fell from 0.0987 to 0.0931 (Brier), better at every position and in every season.
- **Midweek, before the final report.** On Wednesday and Thursday nobody has a game status yet, and a model trained on complete weeks reads "no status" as "cleared": after one practice day it said 86% to play when 65% of those players did. So until a team's final report is out (anyone on it has a game status, or it has three practice days), a separate model for one and for two published days is used, trained on past report weeks cut to their first one or two days, with every outcome. Walk-forward 2021-25 it cut the error (Brier score) from 0.203 to 0.160, better in every season, and its average matches what happened within 1.2 points. A Wednesday DNP now reads about 40% to play (it had been about 80%); a Wednesday full practice barely moves (about 87%).

### Healthy scratches and backup quarterbacks

- **Injury vs healthy scratch: two separate numbers.** The chance to play shown on cards and injury tags is about injuries only (report, practice, ESPN, game-day rulings); a player not on the report reads healthy. A healthy scratch (on the game-day inactive list without an injury) is its own risk, shown as its own tag ("Scratch 30%", from 10%) and never as an injury. The chance he takes the field (injury x healthy scratch x the backup-quarterback rule below) decides who is counted as sitting when touches are handed to teammates, and the expected points in the Fantasy tools.
- **Healthy scratches.** For players not on the report, a second model predicts whether he is on the game-day inactive list (the weekly rosters' active / inactive marks, 2019 on), not whether he takes a snap: a dressed backup who never plays is not a scratch. It uses how often the player has dressed this season, whether he dressed last week, snap share, depth-chart rank, rookie status, how many teammates at his position ahead of him are out, and job security: whether he started his team's first game, how well he has played this season (EPA per opportunity percentile at his position), a first-round rookie behind him, his team's win percentage, his contract's share of the salary cap, and whether it is the final regular-season week (rest days). Job security tested 2021-25: error on players not on the report 0.0765 -> 0.0763 (Brier), mostly running backs; quarterbacks no different, since healthy starting quarterbacks almost never sit (1.4% of weeks).
- **Promoted backup quarterback.** When last game's starting quarterback is out or on the injury report, the healthy quarterback who dressed as his backup last game (the depth chart breaks a tie) is the starter, not a scratch candidate: his healthy-scratch chance is the measured 0.9% (2 of 214 such players were inactive, 2019-25; the model had averaged 3.6% on them and gone as high as 33%). A first version that found him by recent snaps failed: backups barely play, so their snaps can't rank them.
- **Backup quarterback.** The team's No. 2 quarterback on the depth chart (not on the report) plays when the starter sits: his chance = the starter's chance to sit + the starter's chance to play x 23.2% (how often a healthy backup plays anyway: relief, blowouts; 2021-23). Tested 2024-25 on backups whose starter had any chance to sit (371 games): error 0.228 -> 0.196 (Brier), interval excluding zero. Before this the healthy-scratch model scored him as a generic backup unless the starter was ruled out. Naming him by who dressed as the backup instead picked the same player in 198 of 211 games the starter missed (2019-25) and did not test better. Even a correctly named No. 2 played only about 80% of the games his starter missed (sometimes another quarterback starts), so the rule runs somewhat high when the starter is ruled out.

### How it is shown

- **Scenarios, not discounts.** NFL availability is yes or no, so every projection is "if active", with the chance to play shown separately.

## Games

Team ratings drive the Power rankings, each game's expected volume and points, the Matchups battle bars and our own win chances.

### Team ratings

Each play's EPA (how much the play changed the expected score) is modeled as

> EPA = league average + offense strength + defense strength + home edge (+ the quarterback's own effect, for passing)

fit separately for **passing** (dropbacks, including sacks and scrambles), **rushing** (designed runs) and **special teams** (punts, kickoffs and extra points; field goals are not in the team ratings, since a kicker's make rate barely carries from one half of a season to the other). Only regular-season plays are used. Kneels, spikes and two-point tries are removed. Every other play counts, blowouts included: a garbage-time filter (only plays with the offense's win probability between 10% and 90%) was tested and removed, because keeping those plays made game predictions better (margin error 10.64 -> 10.55 points, better in four of five seasons, winners picked 59% -> 61%) with no difference to fantasy projections.

- **Opponent adjustment.** Solving for all teams at once (ridge regression) means a defense that faced weak offenses gets less credit than its raw numbers suggest. Ridge regression shrinks each team, and the home edge, toward average by an amount (alpha) chosen by **time-split cross-validation** on the training seasons: for each week from week 4 on, fit on everything before it, predict each team's EPA per play that week, and never shuffle. Current alphas: pass **{{ L.alpha_pass }}**, rush **{{ L.alpha_rush }}**, special teams **{{ L.alpha_st }}**.
- **Recency.** Plays from this season are weighted with a half-life of **{{ L.hl_team }}** weeks. Last season's plays all carry one flat weight: full weight for week-1 projections (they are all there is), then **{{ L.prior_weight }}** x (**{{ L.prior_decay }}** - week) / ({{ L.prior_decay }} - 1), which is about 0.26 at week 2 and zero by week **{{ L.prior_decay }}**. Older seasons are not used.
- **Quarterbacks.** Passing offense = the team (line, receivers, scheme) + its quarterback + a league-wide effect for inexperienced quarterbacks (fewer than **{{ L.qb_cut }}** career dropbacks, counting pass attempts back to 2006). Its size and sign are learned, not assumed. The team and QB shrinkage amounts are tuned jointly; the QB grid includes "no QB effects at all", so cross-validation could reject them. QB shrinkage alpha **{{ L.alpha_qb }}**, inexperienced-QB effect **{{ L.qb_inexp }}** points per 100 dropbacks. Highest QB effects among quarterbacks with any loaded history (not only current starters): {{ L.top_qbs }}.
- **Home field.** One league-wide edge per unit (passing, rushing and special teams), fit inside the same weighted regression as the ratings. Currently pass **{{ L.home_pass }}**, rush **{{ L.home_rush }}** points per 100 plays. Neutral-site games get none. A division-game home term was tested and is off.
- **Certainty.** Ratings are refit on **{{ L.bootstrap }}** resamples of games (a cluster bootstrap) to measure each passing and running unit's uncertainty. The Power tab simulates each team's net rating from those spreads and shows the middle 80% of simulated ranks. Special-teams and QB uncertainty are not included.

Ratings are estimated per play. On the Power tab they are shown as **points per game versus an average team on a neutral field**: per-play rating x a league-average number of those plays x the points formula's points-per-EPA weight (the points formula, under Games). That way the units add up. Values per 100 plays appear on hover. Higher is better for both offense and defense, because the defense number is flipped so "good" always reads positive.

**What the Power rank means.** How good a team is from here on: its rating averaged over its remaining regular-season games, with the usual starting quarterback (most starts this season) counted by his chance to be back in each game, from the same return-timing model the lookahead weeks use, and his backup for the rest. A one-week absence barely moves a team; a season-ending one counts fully. A starter hurt in his last game is chained from missing this week; one placed on injured reserve sits the league minimum (4 games) or until ESPN's estimated return date, whichever is later. Power shows only this one strength per team: a quarterback out this week or for the next few weeks is already in it, in proportion. Matchups rates each game with that week's expected starter (a this-week rating that isn't shown on Power). Hollow rings on the trend mark weeks rated with a backup. Schedule left rates future opponents the same way. Walk-forward 2021-25, ranking by this rest-of-season strength predicted each team's actual point differential over its remaining games slightly better than ranking by this week's lineup (rank correlation 0.445 vs 0.436; in weeks with a starter out +0.010, interval +0.002 to +0.019), and better than point differential so far (0.424) or win percentage (0.381). Quarterbacks are the only absences the team ratings know (other positions were tested and didn't hold up), so injuries elsewhere don't move it.

**Field position is already accounted for.** EPA judges every play against what was expected from that exact down, distance and field position. A defense handed a short field by its offense's turnover starts the drive already "owing" the high expected points of that spot, and is charged only for what happens beyond that; the turnover itself counts against the offense that committed it. Raw points-allowed would wrongly blame the defense.

### Volume and points

**Volume.** Each team's expected plays come from a regression on its recent plays per game and the plays its opponent allows. Pass rate comes from three things:

- the team's recent pass rate;
- its neutral-situation pass rate over expected (PROE: win probability 20-80%, quarters 1-3);
- game script: the Vegas spread when lines are posted, otherwise the model's own projected margin.

Player projections also use the Vegas total and spread for every game that has both; a game without a line (taken off the board) uses the model's own numbers, game by game. Matchups always uses the model-only version.

**Points.** Expected offensive EPA per game (dropbacks × pass EPA + rushes × rush EPA, against this opponent, including home field and the starting QB), plus the special-teams edge, converts to points through a calibration learned on the 6 seasons before: {{ L.points_model }}. It uses a longer window than other components because two seasons pin down the special-teams weight poorly.

**The model publishes win chances only.** Point spreads on the site are always the market's. The model's own margin (team ratings, projected starting QBs and pace; never Vegas) is never shown. It only sets game script for the projected pass rate, and the build checks that the battle-bar impacts add up to it.

### Battle bars (Matchups)

These break the matchup down in EPA per 100 plays:

- each offense's rating minus the opposing defense's, with the total weighting passing and running by that offense's projected dropbacks and runs;
- special teams per 100 special-teams plays;
- the home-field edge per scrimmage play.

Pace is listed as projected plays and pass rate. Bar length is the edge's impact on the game through the points formula, on one scale for every row:

- offense rows: edge x projected plays x points per EPA;
- special teams: edge x league-average special-teams plays x twice the fitted special-teams weight.

A full bar is the 95th percentile of offense-vs-defense impact across every pairing of that week's ratings. So equal numbers can draw different bars, and each special-teams play counts more than a scrimmage play because of field position.

### Win chances

- **Vegas win chances.** The spread becomes a win probability through P(win) = 1 / (1 + e^(-k x spread)), with k fit every run on every earlier finished game with a line (playoffs included, ties excluded).
- **Our model's win chance** = 1 / (1 + e^(-x)), where x = **{{ L.wm_week_home }}** for the home team (0 at a neutral site) + **{{ L.wm_week_rank_gap }}** per place of advantage in this week's rank (Power's rating with each team's projected quarterback for this week; not shown on Power) + **{{ L.wm_week_elo_per_100 }}** per 100 points of Elo advantage. The playoff odds play out future games, where the lineup isn't known yet, so they use Power's rest-of-season rank with their own weights ({{ L.wm_home }} home, {{ L.wm_rank_gap }} per place, {{ L.wm_elo_per_100 }} per 100 Elo).
- **Elo** is a rating from final scores only, playoffs included. Every team starts at 1505 in the first loaded season. After each game it moves by K = **{{ L.wm_elo_k }}** x (result - expected) x a margin multiplier, ln(|margin| + 1) x 2.2 / (winner's Elo edge x 0.001 + 2.2), so bigger wins move it more but a favorite's expected wins move it less. The expected result counts **{{ L.wm_elo_hfa }}** Elo points of home field (none at neutral sites). Each offseason, ratings are pulled **{{ L.wm_elo_revert }}** of the way back to 1505.
- **How the blend was chosen.** The live Elo settings and the three weights are fit once by `tools/elo_eval.py` on every season through 2026 and stored in configuration. The backtest refits them each test year on earlier seasons only. Elo's settings were stable across those years. Walk-forward on 2022-26 games with a line (Brier score, the average squared miss; 0 would be perfect): this blend 0.2228, Power rank + home 0.2292, the model's margin alone 0.2353, Vegas 0.2111, always the home team 0.249. Elo adds what the play-by-play ratings miss: actual results and memory across seasons. Rating each team with the quarterback starting that game, rather than last week's, improved it again (0.2220 -> 0.2203; 0.2214 with the rest-of-season rank), so Matchups uses this week's rank. On a larger sample (`tools/rank_predictor.py`), the rank also beat the rating gap it comes from (0.2291 vs 0.2316), because the size of extreme rating gaps is unreliable. Nothing tested has added anything to the market's own chance (`tools/darts.py`: 28 pre-game signals including QB tiers, rest, travel, cold weather and referees). So the Matchups headline is the market's chance whenever a line exists, and the model's only before lines post.
- **Tossups.** A game is a tossup when the favorite's chance, rounded, is under **{{ L.tossup }}%**. That is the largest cut-off below which the market's favorites won at a rate a coin flip explains (54% +/- 6%, 90% interval, on 2022-26). It falls at a spread under 3 points; 3-point favorites won about 61%. The track record at the end of this page shows, for every confidence level, how often the favorite actually won, using chances fit only on earlier seasons.

- **Win chances.** The market's win chances beat the model's, and no blend with the model helped. Against the spread, the model's lean covered 51.7% (break-even 52.4%), and bigger model-vs-Vegas disagreements did worse. Margin corrections (flat recalibration, team "mistake memory") did not help.
- **Power rank.** It beat the rating gap it comes from and was adopted.
- **28 pre-game signals.** Only Elo helped, and it shipped as part of our win chance. The rest did nothing: QB tiers, record, point differential, close-game luck, turnovers, streaks, rest, byes, travel, prime time, division games, cold weather, surface, new coaches, referees.
- **Referees.** No referee or referee-team effect on spread or total results persists from one set of games to the next.
- **Prior-season carryover in team ratings.** A quick three-season check found more of last season helps weeks 2-8 but hurts later weeks. Not adopted; parked for a fuller test.

Ideas that sounded right but did not improve out-of-sample accuracy are switched off, not shipped:

- **Team-specific home field.** Team home edges do not persist year to year (correlation -0.02 over 567 team-seasons from 2005-25; Seattle's edge was +5.7 points in 2005-12 and about zero since 2019). Travel distance and time zones showed no effect. A division-game term made cross-validation worse.
- **Defensive and offensive-line absences.** Stat-weighted defender values overfit. Counting only absences knowable before kickoff, no position group (cornerbacks included) or offensive line showed a reliable effect beyond the team rating.
- **Time of possession / pace and drive sustainability in the volume model.** Neutral-situation seconds per play and expected EPA per play (offense vs this defense, both ways) did not improve out-of-sample team play counts (plays MAE 6.88 -> 6.87-6.92 on 2025, 6.75 -> 6.78-6.80 on 2026). Drive outcomes are already inside EPA, and time of possession mostly reflects game script, which the Vegas spread captures.

## Playoffs

Playoff odds come from playing out the rest of the season thousands of times with the same win chances as Matchups.

### Playoff odds (Teams > Playoffs)

- **Method.** `tools/playoff_odds.py` plays out the rest of the season 10,000 times. Finished games count as played. Each remaining game uses the Matchups chance: the market's when a line is posted, otherwise our model's (above), with ranks and Elo held where they are now. Every simulated season is seeded with the NFL tiebreakers (`nfledge/standings.py`) and the playoffs are played to the end in the real format (the 1 seed's bye, reseeding after the wild-card round, home field to the higher seed, a neutral-site Super Bowl).
- **Drift.** Team strength isn't frozen for the rest of a season: injuries, trades and form all move it. Each simulated season gives every team one strength shift, drawn from a normal distribution with standard deviation 0.9 x sqrt(share of the season left) on the log-odds scale, and applies it to all of that team's remaining games. Without drift the odds were overconfident: teams given 85-95% made the playoffs 76% of the time in 2024-25. The size was pre-registered and chosen on 2021-23 from 0, 0.3, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2 and 1.5. On 2024-25, which the fit never saw, it lowered the make-playoffs Brier from 0.1344 to 0.1273.
- **Track record** (walk-forward, 2021-25, odds as of every week, using that week's Power ranks and lines; 1,000 simulated seasons per week): make-playoffs Brier 0.138 vs 0.246 for the base rate; win the division 0.118 vs 0.188; first-round bye 0.038 vs 0.059; win the Super Bowl 0.028 vs 0.030 (barely better than the base rate: one champion in 32 is hard to call). Calibration: each band of predicted chances from 0-5% to 95-100% made the playoffs within 2.3 points of its average prediction. The odds sharpen as the season goes: Brier 0.206 in weeks 1-4, 0.067 in weeks 15-18. The Playoffs tab shows the full table.

### Rooting guide

For each of this week's unplayed games, the rooting guide asks how much the result changes a team's chances (make the playoffs, win the division, get the top seed). It takes 2,000 of the simulated seasons and replays each one twice with only that game's result flipped, so the "if the home team wins" and "if the away team wins" chances come from the same seasons and the difference is that game's own effect (splitting seasons by who happened to win would mix in noise from every other game). Every game is listed with its swing in all three chances. Games are ranked by the chance the team is racing for (making the playoffs; once that is settled, the division; then the top seed); swings under half a point or within simulation noise are greyed, and a game with a clear swing in any of the three is listed under "Root for". A game between two teams of the other conference still counts: its result can change a tiebreaker such as strength of victory or of schedule, though rarely (one flipped season in 2,000 is 0.05 points, so most such games show 0.0). Best and worst case: all of this week's games going the team's preferred way, or all the other way.

## Grades, kickers and defenses

This area covers the Edge grade and durability on player cards and the kicker and team-defense projections on the Fantasy tab.

### Edge grade (player cards)

The grade measures output, not style: fantasy points per game in the site's scoring, over this season and the two before (weighted 1, 0.6 and 0.3), pulled toward the average of qualified players at his position by a number of pseudo-games measured from how much a player's points vary game to game, so a short hot streak doesn't make an elite grade. It is shown as a percentile (0-99) among the players at his position who met the NFL's qualifying minimums that season. A second number grades that season alone, and the stat chips beside it are his per-opportunity percentiles (for example yards per target), shown to explain the grade, not to make it. Players short of the minimums but with a real role are graded on the games where they played at least half the snaps, against the qualified pool. Seasons before 2018 use the same output formula from box-score stats (the per-opportunity stat chips need data that starts in 2018). The game log's Grd column grades single games the same way and on the same 50-99 scale, by output: a game's fantasy points against every regular-season game that season by qualified players at his position (also shown as a strip of this season's games in the grade sheet), so a 22-point game grades the same in a quiet week as in a high-scoring one (Rk, beside it, is the finish within that week). While a season is in progress, last season's games from the weeks not played yet fill out the comparison, so early-season grades aren't measured against five weeks alone. Playoff games are graded against that season's regular-season games.

Individual defenders get the same grade, by output: IDP fantasy points per game under Sleeper's default IDP123 scoring (solo tackle and tackle for loss 2, assisted tackle and QB hit 1, forced fumble, fumble recovery and pass defended 3, sack and interception 6), plus our own values for a touchdown 6, safety 2 and blocked kick 3, in three groups by listed position: defensive linemen, linebackers, defensive backs. A defender qualifies with a real role that season: from 2012, an average of 40% or more of the defensive snaps over his team's games; before 2012 (no snap counts), a role (2+ tackles, a sack, interception or pass defended) in half its games. Spot starters (2+ games at half the snaps) are graded from those games against the qualified pool. The stat chips are box-score rates plus, from 2018, Pro Football Reference's pressures, coverage allowed (yards per target, passer rating) and missed-tackle rate. The game log's Grd column grades a defender's single games the same way: his IDP points against every regular-season game that season by qualified defenders in his group. The IDP points shown on cards, game logs and the Stats tab follow the scoring panel's IDP weights (IDP123 until changed, or a connected Sleeper league's own).

### Durability (player cards)

Durability is games missed injured per 1,000 snaps on the field since 2013 (offensive snaps for quarterbacks, backs, receivers, tight ends and linemen; defensive snaps for defenders). A game counts as missed when he was on the injury report or a reserve list and not active: from 2019 that means on the game-day inactive list, so a player who dressed but never took a snap is available, not missed; before 2019, no snap. Healthy scratches and suspensions don't count. Players with little time on the field are pulled toward their position's rate (by 250 snaps; 2,000 for tight ends), and the percentile ranks him among players at his position with 1,000 or more snaps; below that he is not ranked. Kickers, punters and long snappers keep the older score, the share of their team's games they were not out injured. Per snap rather than per game because a backup who rarely plays rarely gets hurt, which made backups look durable: judged by how many snaps players lasted before their next injury-missed game, the per-snap score was better every season 2020-25 and at every position (see What we tried).

## Rule changes and scoring eras

League scoring isn't constant. For quarterbacks, running backs, receivers and tight ends together (half PPR), it was 111-119 points per team-game in 1999-2003, climbed through the passing boom to a peak of 138 in 2020, and has fallen since (125-131), moving about 3% from one season to the next. Rule changes explain part of it:

- **Passing rules and their enforcement.** 2004's illegal-contact emphasis (passing yards per team-game 214 to 225 in one season), quarterback protections (2006, 2009), defenseless-receiver protections (2010-11), and the 2018 catch-rule and roughing changes. Most of these arrive as officiating emphasis, so their size can't be known in advance.
- **Field position from kickoffs.** Kickoffs from the 35 (2011), touchbacks to the 25 (2016), fair catches as touchbacks (2023), then the dynamic kickoff with touchbacks to the 30 (2024) and the 35 (2025), which moved where drives start by about five yards. Our projections ran 5.5% low in 2024.
- **Scoring and structure.** Extra points from 33 yards (2015: more misses, more two-point tries), a 17th game (2021), overtime possession for both teams (playoffs 2022, regular season 2025). 2020's empty stadiums (not a rule) briefly erased home-field advantage.

**How the model handles it.** Projections, ranges and chances to play are per game and learned from recent seasons, with Vegas totals (which price a new rule in from day one) as inputs. Grades, game grades and finishes are percentiles within each season or week, so they compare across eras on their own. Season totals don't: a 17-game season adds about 6% with no change per game, so compare across eras per game. We tested scaling projections by how this season's scoring runs against them: it fixed the level but not individual accuracy (see Projections, What we tried), and a rule-change input was not built: about five cases since 1999, each different, would make it a hand-set guess.

## Under the hood

How the data flows, how the model is validated, how this page stays current, and the live formulas.

### The full pipeline

NFL Edge projects weekly fantasy points for QBs, RBs, WRs and TEs and rates every team, using only free data. Every number on this site comes from the pipeline below. Values shown in **bold blue** are filled in live from the current build, so they always match what produced this week's report.

**Pipeline, start to finish:** load and cache data, then fit opponent-adjusted team ratings. From those, predict each game's volume and score, project each player's share of that volume and his efficiency, and adjust for availability and injuries. Finally, blend with the expert consensus where that has been shown to help, and attach calibrated ranges. Learned components train only on seasons *before* the one being projected or tested, mostly the **{{ L.train_seasons }}** seasons before it. The exceptions use longer history:

- the points formula: up to 6 earlier seasons;
- the practice-availability model: every season since 2018;
- the injury-return model: every loaded season, including this season's finished absences;
- the Vegas win-chance curve and Elo: every earlier game.

Team ratings use this season's completed games plus a fading share of last season's. A few settings are fixed values chosen by testing on 2021-2026 history: the win-chance blend's weights, the tossup line, and tuned knobs such as half-lives and the wind penalty.

### Validation

Every validation number on this site is **walk-forward**: projections for a week use only data available before it, and learned components are trained only on earlier seasons. Changes are adopted only if a 5-season walk-forward test (2021-2025, 95% confidence intervals over season-weeks) shows a gain that excludes zero.
